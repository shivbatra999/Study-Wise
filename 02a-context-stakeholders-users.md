## 2.1 Product perspective and context

![Context diagram](diagrams/context.png)

The boundary sits around the part that turns a student's files and questions into cited answers and quizzes. That is the part with no off-the-shelf equivalent that respects CON-01, so it is ours to build. The language model and the runtime that executes it are deliberately outside the box: we call them but do not build or train them, which is why they appear as an external system and as dependency A2. The student's file system is external because Study-Wise only reads files the student chooses and never modifies them. The Internet and cloud AI services are drawn outside the boundary with no interface at all, because CON-01 forbids one. Instructors never interact with the system, but their material enters through the file-system interface, which is why they are stakeholders without being actors.

## 2.3 User characteristics

**Technical students (CS, CE, SE and other STEM majors):** Comfortable installing desktop software and following a README; all five interviewees already use cloud AI assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) to study, and all five have received wrong answers from them. Some catch errors by checking their slides or noticing inconsistent follow-up answers; others find errors hard to spot. They therefore need to be told when an answer has no support in their materials, rather than receive a confident guess. Three of five interviewees said the slide or page an answer came from is very important and that they would open it to check. They use personal laptops, often on battery: 8 of 10 survey respondents study on battery at least some of the time. They use the system hardest in the days before an exam, when they are short on time and impatient with slow answers (QR-01).

**Non-technical students (all other majors):** Can import files and type questions but cannot be assumed to use a terminal, install a runtime, or diagnose why a model is not running. The survey shows this is a skill gap rather than a major: 4 of 10 respondents have never installed software from a README or terminal, and these include a Computer Science student. This user class is why FR-15 exists and why first-time setup is a use case of its own (UC-06, Q5).

**Constraints from both classes:** Survey respondents' laptops range from 8 GB or less (3 of 10) to 32 GB or more (3 of 10); 6 of 10 have 16 GB or more, so assumption A1 holds, but only narrowly. 9 of 10 run Windows 11 or macOS 14 or later (CON-03); only 3 of 10 confirmed a separate graphics card; none reported less than 10 GB of free storage. 2 of 10 respondents use assistive features while studying (zoom or large text, speech-to-text, a screen reader). We have not yet written an accessibility requirement and record this as open question Q10 rather than invent a threshold. The samples are small and mostly Computer Science students (7 of 10 survey respondents), so these results are indicative, not conclusive.
